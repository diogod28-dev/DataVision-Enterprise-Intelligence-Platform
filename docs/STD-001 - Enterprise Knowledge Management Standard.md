## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Purpose

This document establishes the Enterprise Knowledge Management Standard for DataVision Solutions. It defines the principles, structures, responsibilities, lifecycle controls, metadata requirements and governance practices required to create, maintain and improve enterprise Knowledge Assets within the DataVision Enterprise Knowledge System (DEKS).

This standard is the governing reference for every future Knowledge Asset created within the organisation. It ensures that knowledge is captured consistently, organised deliberately, maintained responsibly and made reusable across business, product, operational, research and client contexts.

### 1.2 Objectives

The objectives of this standard are to:

- define knowledge as a strategic organisational asset;
- establish a consistent structure for the DataVision Enterprise Knowledge System (DEKS);
- define Knowledge Asset categories, ownership and lifecycle stages;
- specify mandatory metadata and relationship standards;
- reduce duplication by promoting authoritative sources and cross-referencing;
- support long-term knowledge preservation beyond individual projects, employees and technologies;
- define the appropriate role of AI-assisted knowledge creation; and
- enable continuous improvement through review, audit and refinement.

### 1.3 Scope

This standard applies to all Knowledge Assets created, maintained or archived by DataVision Solutions. This includes standards, templates, blueprints, research notes, product knowledge, client assets, operational documents, innovation records and archived enterprise knowledge.

This standard does not prescribe detailed implementation tooling, repository technology or document automation methods. Those decisions may be defined in supporting standards, procedures or templates.

### 1.4 Audience

This document is intended for:

- founders and leadership;
- knowledge owners and knowledge stewards;
- product, delivery, engineering and operations teams;
- contributors who create or maintain Knowledge Assets;
- reviewers and approvers;
- AI-assisted documentation contributors; and
- future personnel responsible for maintaining the Enterprise Knowledge Base.

### 1.5 Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| DataVision Enterprise Knowledge System (DEKS) | The governed enterprise system for DataVision Solutions knowledge. It functions like a well-designed database for organisational knowledge. |
| Enterprise Knowledge Base | The governed repository of DataVision Solutions knowledge within DEKS. |
| Knowledge Asset | A governed document or artefact that captures reusable organisational knowledge. In the knowledge database analogy, Knowledge Assets function like tables or views. |
| Master Index | The authoritative index of Knowledge Assets. It functions like a data dictionary for the Enterprise Knowledge Base. |
| Template | A reusable structure for creating a specific type of Knowledge Asset. Templates function like schemas. |
| Metadata | Standard descriptive information assigned to a Knowledge Asset. Metadata functions like data dictionary attributes. |
| Cross Reference | A documented relationship between Knowledge Assets. Cross References function like foreign keys. |
| Knowledge Governance | The rules, roles and controls used to manage enterprise knowledge. It functions like database administration. |
| Knowledge Lifecycle | The defined progression of a Knowledge Asset from idea to archive. It functions like a record lifecycle. |
| Knowledge Debt | Organisational knowledge that is missing, outdated, duplicated, inconsistent or no longer trustworthy. |

## 2 Enterprise Knowledge Philosophy

### 2.1 Knowledge as a Strategic Asset

Knowledge is one of the organisation's most valuable strategic assets. It includes decisions, methods, standards, patterns, research, client context, product thinking, operational practices and lessons learned. When knowledge is captured and governed effectively, it enables execution, reduces rework, improves quality and preserves organisational capability.

Documentation is not paperwork. Documentation is the process of capturing organisational knowledge in a form that can be searched, reused, maintained and improved. A Knowledge Asset must therefore have a clear purpose, defined ownership, appropriate metadata, relationships with other Knowledge Assets and a lifecycle.

Knowledge must not depend on one individual. It must outlive projects, employees and technologies.

### 2.2 Single Source of Truth

Every Knowledge Asset must have one authoritative source. Copies, extracts and summaries may exist for operational convenience, but they must reference the authoritative source and must not become unmanaged alternatives.

DEKS should function like a well-designed database. Each Knowledge Asset should have a defined purpose and position within the broader knowledge architecture. Duplication should be avoided unless there is a clear governance reason.

### 2.3 Knowledge Preservation

Knowledge preservation ensures that decisions, practices and learning remain available after the original context has changed. Projects may end, technologies may be replaced and personnel may change roles, but the organisation must retain its ability to understand what was decided, why it was decided and how it should be applied.

### 2.4 Knowledge Reuse

Knowledge must be structured for reuse. Standards, templates, blueprints and reference materials should reduce repeated effort and enable consistent execution. Knowledge Assets should be written so they can be applied across future projects where appropriate, rather than serving only a single immediate task.

### 2.5 Knowledge Quality

Knowledge quality is determined by accuracy, clarity, completeness, consistency, relevance and trustworthiness. A Knowledge Asset should be sufficiently precise to support execution and sufficiently maintainable to remain useful over time.

### 2.6 Knowledge Accessibility

Knowledge must be searchable and accessible to authorised audiences. Metadata, naming conventions, classification, indexing and cross-references are essential because they allow users to locate the right Knowledge Asset at the right time.

### 2.7 Enterprise Search Philosophy

Enterprise search is a core design concern for DEKS. Knowledge is only valuable when authorised users can find, understand and apply it at the point of need.

Metadata, keywords, document IDs, classification, cross-references and the Master Index work together to improve discoverability. Document IDs provide stable identifiers. Metadata and keywords support search and filtering. Classification clarifies access and handling expectations. Cross-references connect related Knowledge Assets and reduce search dead ends. The Master Index provides the authoritative navigation layer and functions like the data dictionary for the knowledge database.

### 2.8 Knowledge Evolution

Enterprise knowledge evolves. Standards, templates, product thinking, research and operating models must be reviewed and refined as the organisation learns. A Knowledge Asset is not complete simply because it has been published; it remains part of a managed lifecycle.

### 2.9 Knowledge Independence

Knowledge independence means that organisational capability is not locked inside individual memory, private files or unmanaged conversations. Knowledge Assets must preserve essential context so that future users can understand, apply and improve the knowledge without depending exclusively on the original contributor.

## 3 Enterprise Knowledge Architecture

### 3.1 Overview

The DataVision Enterprise Knowledge System (DEKS) is the structured environment through which DataVision Solutions captures, governs and improves organisational knowledge. It should be treated as a knowledge database, with defined categories, metadata, templates, relationships and governance controls.

> Diagram Placeholder - Enterprise Knowledge Architecture

### 3.2 Standards

Standards define mandatory or governing expectations. They establish how the organisation manages knowledge, designs solutions, applies architecture practices, handles operational procedures or governs recurring enterprise activities.

### 3.3 Templates

Templates define reusable structures for creating Knowledge Assets. In the knowledge database analogy, templates function like schemas. They improve consistency, reduce creation effort and ensure that required metadata and sections are included.

### 3.4 Blueprint

Blueprint Knowledge Assets describe high-level enterprise, product, platform or solution structures. They may define target architecture, conceptual models, strategic direction or major design patterns.

### 3.5 Brand Book

Brand Book assets define the approved identity, tone, presentation conventions and brand-related knowledge for DataVision Solutions. These assets support consistency across internal and external materials.

### 3.6 Research & Innovation

Research & Innovation assets capture ideas, investigations, concept papers, technology evaluations, proof of concepts and future opportunities. These assets preserve early thinking even when implementation may occur months or years later.

### 3.7 Products

Product Knowledge Assets describe product concepts, requirements, capabilities, features, roadmaps, architecture considerations and operating knowledge related to DataVision Solutions products.

### 3.8 Client Assets

Client Assets capture client-specific knowledge, subject to classification and access controls. They may include approved context, engagement learning, solution notes or delivery references relevant to client work.

### 3.9 Operations

Operations assets define recurring business, delivery, administrative and support processes. They help ensure consistent execution and reduce dependency on informal process knowledge.

### 3.10 Archive

The Archive preserves Knowledge Assets that are no longer active but remain valuable for historical, legal, operational or learning purposes. Archived assets must retain sufficient metadata to explain their status and relationship to current knowledge.

Knowledge Asset classification provides a consistent way to identify the family, purpose and governance expectations of each asset. Classification supports searchability, ownership, lifecycle management and cross-reference integrity.

### 4.2 Knowledge Asset Categories

Additional document families may be introduced as the organisation matures. New families must have a defined purpose, naming convention, ownership model and relationship to existing families before they are adopted.

## 5 Knowledge Asset Lifecycle

### 5.1 Lifecycle Overview

Every Knowledge Asset must move through a defined lifecycle. The lifecycle ensures that ideas are captured, drafts are reviewed, approved assets are published and outdated knowledge is maintained or archived.

In the knowledge database analogy, the Knowledge Asset lifecycle functions like a record lifecycle. Each asset has a state, owner, review history and maintenance responsibility.

> Diagram Placeholder - Knowledge Asset Lifecycle

### 5.2 Lifecycle Stages

| Stage | Description | Primary Responsibilities |
|---|---|---|
| Idea | A potential Knowledge Asset is identified. | Capture the concept, assign a preliminary owner and determine whether it should become a Knowledge Asset. |
| Research | Supporting information, context and rationale are gathered. | Validate the need, identify related assets and record sources or assumptions. |
| Draft | The Knowledge Asset is written in the appropriate template or structure. | Prepare content, apply metadata and identify relationships. |
| Review | The draft is evaluated for accuracy, clarity, completeness and alignment. | Review content, resolve issues and confirm readiness for approval. |
| Approved | The Knowledge Asset is formally accepted by the approval authority. | Confirm ownership, version, classification and approval record. |
| Published | The Knowledge Asset is made available to its authorised audience. | Add to the Master Index, update cross-references and communicate availability where appropriate. |
| Maintained | The Knowledge Asset is periodically reviewed and updated. | Monitor relevance, update content and manage version history. |
| Archived | The Knowledge Asset is removed from active use but retained for reference. | Mark status, preserve context and link to any superseding assets. |

### 5.3 Lifecycle Responsibilities

Lifecycle responsibility must be assigned before a Knowledge Asset is published. At minimum, each asset must have an owner responsible for relevance, review and maintenance. Reviewers and approvers may vary by asset type, classification and organisational importance.

## 6 Metadata Standard

### 6.1 Purpose

Metadata is mandatory for every Knowledge Asset. It allows DEKS to operate like a well-designed database by enabling search, filtering, ownership tracking, lifecycle control and relationship management.

Metadata functions like data dictionary attributes. Without metadata, knowledge becomes difficult to govern, search and trust.

### 6.2 Standard Metadata Fields

| Metadata Field | Requirement | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Document ID | Mandatory | Unique identifier assigned according to the relevant document family. |
| Title | Mandatory | Official title of the Knowledge Asset. |
| Version | Mandatory | Current version of the Knowledge Asset. |
| Status | Mandatory | Lifecycle status, such as Draft, Approved, Published, Maintained or Archived. |
| Owner | Mandatory | Person, role or organisational function accountable for the asset. |
| Prepared By | Recommended | Person or contributor who prepared the current version. |
| Reviewed By | Recommended | Person or role that reviewed the content. |
| Approved By | Required for approval | Person or authority that approved the asset. |
| Created Date | Mandatory | Date the asset was first created. |
| Updated Date | Mandatory after first update | Date the asset was last materially updated. |
| Review Date | Mandatory for governed assets | Next scheduled review date. |
| Classification | Mandatory | Information classification, such as Internal or Restricted. |
| Knowledge Domain | Recommended | Primary knowledge domain represented by the asset. Example domains include Business, Architecture, Cloud, Database, Security, AI, Operations, Finance, Legal and Research. |
| Keywords | Recommended | Search terms that improve discoverability. |
| Parent Documents | Conditional | Higher-level assets that govern or contain this asset. |
| Child Documents | Conditional | Lower-level assets governed by or derived from this asset. |
| Dependencies | Conditional | Assets or decisions required for this asset to remain valid. |
| Related Knowledge Assets | Recommended | Non-hierarchical assets with relevant relationships. |
| Purpose | Mandatory | Reason the Knowledge Asset exists. |
| Audience | Mandatory | Intended users or stakeholder groups. |

### 6.3 Knowledge Domain

Knowledge Domain identifies the primary area of organisational knowledge represented by a Knowledge Asset. It improves search, filtering, stewardship, auditability and repository analysis.

Example Knowledge Domains include Business, Architecture, Cloud, Database, Security, AI, Operations, Finance, Legal and Research. Additional domains may be introduced when they improve classification and discoverability without fragmenting the repository.

### 6.4 Metadata Quality Expectations

Metadata must be accurate, current and meaningful. Generic keywords, missing ownership and unclear status values reduce the trustworthiness of the Enterprise Knowledge Base. Metadata should be reviewed whenever the Knowledge Asset is updated.

## 7 Relationships & Cross References

### 7.1 Purpose

Knowledge Assets must reference one another through clear relationship types. Cross-references reduce duplication by allowing each Knowledge Asset to focus on its authoritative purpose while linking to related knowledge where needed.

In the knowledge database analogy, cross-references function like foreign keys. They connect records across the Enterprise Knowledge Base and allow users to navigate from one Knowledge Asset to another with confidence.

### 7.2 Relationship Types

| Relationship Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Parent | A higher-level Knowledge Asset that governs, contains or contextualises another asset. |
| Child | A lower-level Knowledge Asset governed by, derived from or contained under another asset. |
| Depends On | An asset that must remain valid for the current asset to remain accurate or usable. |
| Related To | A non-hierarchical relationship indicating relevant supporting or adjacent knowledge. |
| Supersedes | Indicates that the current asset replaces an earlier Knowledge Asset. |
| Referenced By | Indicates other assets that cite or rely on the current asset. |
| Future References | Identifies planned or expected future relationships that have not yet been created. |

### 7.3 Cross-Reference Standards

Cross-references should be specific enough to support navigation and governance. Where possible, references should include the Document ID and title. References must be updated when Knowledge Assets are renamed, superseded, archived or replaced.

### 7.4 Duplication Reduction

Cross-referencing reduces duplication by allowing knowledge to be captured once and reused elsewhere. If a concept is already governed by an existing Knowledge Asset, future documents should reference that asset rather than restating the same content in full. This supports consistency and reduces Knowledge Debt.

## 8 Governance

### 8.1 Governance Purpose

Knowledge Governance defines the accountability, controls and practices required to keep the Enterprise Knowledge Base trustworthy. In the knowledge database analogy, Knowledge Governance functions like database administration.

Governance ensures that Knowledge Assets have owners, reviews, approval paths, maintenance responsibilities and quality expectations.

### 8.2 Roles & Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Owner | Accountable for the relevance, accuracy and maintenance of a Knowledge Asset. |
| Prepared By | Creates or updates the Knowledge Asset in accordance with applicable standards and templates. |
| Reviewer | Reviews the asset for accuracy, completeness, clarity and alignment with related knowledge. |
| Approver | Provides formal approval according to the required authority level. |
| Knowledge Steward | Supports repository consistency, metadata quality, indexing and lifecycle discipline. |
| Publisher | Makes approved Knowledge Assets available to authorised audiences and updates the Master Index. |
| User | Applies Knowledge Assets appropriately and identifies issues, gaps or improvement opportunities. |

### 8.3 Ownership

Every Knowledge Asset must have an owner. Ownership may belong to a person, role or organisational function, but it must be clear who is accountable for maintaining the asset and deciding when updates are required.

### 8.4 Review

Reviews must assess whether the Knowledge Asset is accurate, complete, clear, useful and aligned with related assets. Reviewers should also identify duplication, missing metadata, unclear relationships and potential Knowledge Debt.

### 8.5 Approval

Approval confirms that a Knowledge Asset is accepted for its intended purpose and audience. Approval authority must be appropriate to the asset's importance, classification and organisational impact.

### 8.6 Publishing

Published Knowledge Assets must be discoverable through the Master Index or other approved navigation structures. Publishing should include status confirmation, metadata validation and cross-reference updates.

### 8.7 Maintenance

Maintenance includes periodic review, updates, correction of issues, relationship management and version control. A published Knowledge Asset must not be treated as static unless it has been explicitly archived.

### 8.8 Knowledge Stewardship

Knowledge Stewardship protects the integrity of the Enterprise Knowledge Base. Stewards help ensure that naming, metadata, classification, lifecycle status and repository structure remain consistent.

### 8.9 Knowledge Quality

Knowledge quality is a governance responsibility. Quality expectations include clarity, accuracy, relevance, completeness, consistency, traceability and usability.

### 8.10 Knowledge Audits

Knowledge audits should be conducted periodically to identify missing, duplicated, outdated or low-trust assets. Audit outcomes should be used to reduce Knowledge Debt and improve repository reliability.

## 9 AI Collaboration

### 9.1 Purpose

AI may assist in the creation, review, organisation and refinement of Knowledge Assets. AI is a productivity aid and should be used within clear governance boundaries.

AI does not replace human ownership, judgement, review or approval.

### 9.2 AI Draft Generation

AI may be used to generate initial drafts, outlines, summaries, metadata suggestions, cross-reference suggestions or alternative wording. AI-generated content must be reviewed before it becomes part of an approved Knowledge Asset.

### 9.3 Human Review

Human review is mandatory for governed Knowledge Assets. Reviewers must evaluate factual accuracy, business relevance, completeness, tone, classification, intellectual property considerations and alignment with existing knowledge.

### 9.4 Traceability

Where AI materially contributes to a Knowledge Asset, the contribution should be traceable according to the organisation's documentation practices. Traceability may include notes on source inputs, assumptions, review actions or version history.

### 9.5 Approval

AI-generated or AI-assisted content must follow the same approval process as human-drafted content. Approval applies to the final reviewed asset, not to the AI output itself.

### 9.6 Quality Assurance

AI-assisted Knowledge Assets must be checked for accuracy, consistency, completeness and duplication. AI outputs may contain plausible but incorrect information and must not be treated as authoritative without review.

### 9.7 Ethical Use

AI must be used responsibly. Contributors must consider confidentiality, intellectual property, data protection, bias, attribution and the sensitivity of source material. Restricted or confidential information must only be handled through approved methods.

### 9.8 Continuous Improvement

AI can support continuous improvement by identifying inconsistencies, proposing metadata, finding duplication and assisting with repository maintenance. These suggestions remain subject to human judgement and governance.

## 10 Research & Innovation

### 10.1 Purpose

The Research & Innovation repository captures early ideas, investigations and emerging opportunities. Ideas may exist for months or years before implementation. Preserving them allows the organisation to retain strategic thinking, revisit earlier analysis and connect future work to past learning.

Research and innovation knowledge should be captured early, even when the idea is incomplete. The purpose is not to force immediate execution, but to prevent valuable thinking from being lost.

> Diagram Placeholder - Research & Innovation Pipeline

### 10.2 Research Notes

Research Notes capture observations, references, questions, assumptions and early analysis. They may be informal at first, but should still include sufficient metadata to support future discovery.

### 10.3 Concept Papers

Concept Papers describe potential opportunities, business ideas, product concepts, architectural approaches or strategic themes. They should explain the purpose, context, potential value, known risks and open questions.

### 10.4 Architecture Sketches

Architecture Sketches capture early structural thinking about systems, products, platforms or operating models. They may precede formal blueprints and should be linked to related research and future product assets where relevant.

### 10.5 Future Products

Future Product assets capture potential product opportunities before they become active initiatives. They may include problem statements, target users, capability concepts, constraints, assumptions and dependencies.

### 10.6 Technology Evaluations

Technology Evaluations assess technologies, tools, platforms or methods for possible future use. They should document evaluation criteria, findings, risks, limitations and recommended next steps.

### 10.7 Proofs of Concept

Proofs of Concept capture experimental validation of an idea, technology or approach. They should document objectives, scope, results, limitations and implications for future work.

### 10.8 Innovation Pipeline

The Innovation Pipeline provides a managed view of ideas from initial capture through research, evaluation, prototyping and potential implementation. It helps the organisation maintain visibility of future opportunities without requiring premature commitment.

## 11 Continuous Improvement

### 11.1 Purpose

The Enterprise Knowledge Base must continuously improve. Knowledge becomes less reliable when it is not reviewed, updated or connected to current practice. Continuous improvement ensures that the repository remains useful, accurate and trusted.

### 11.2 Periodic Reviews

Knowledge Assets should be reviewed at defined intervals based on their importance, risk, classification and rate of change. Reviews should confirm whether the asset remains accurate, relevant and properly related to other assets.

### 11.3 Knowledge Audits

Knowledge audits assess repository health. Audits may identify missing assets, duplicated content, outdated guidance, broken references, unclear ownership, inconsistent metadata or assets that should be archived.

### 11.4 Repository Maintenance

Repository maintenance includes organising assets, updating indexes, correcting metadata, validating cross-references, archiving obsolete content and ensuring that templates remain fit for purpose.

### 11.5 Lessons Learned

Lessons learned should be captured after significant projects, decisions, incidents, research efforts or operational changes. Lessons must be transformed into reusable Knowledge Assets where they have ongoing value.

### 11.6 Retrospectives

Retrospectives provide structured reflection on what worked, what did not work and what should change. Relevant outcomes should be linked to standards, templates, operations assets, product assets or research records.

### 11.7 Knowledge Debt Reduction

Knowledge Debt is organisational knowledge that is missing, outdated, duplicated or no longer trustworthy. It reduces execution quality and increases dependency on informal memory.

Knowledge Debt should be identified, prioritised and reduced through review, consolidation, archiving, rewriting or creation of missing Knowledge Assets.

### 11.8 Continuous Refinement

Continuous refinement means that Knowledge Assets should improve as the organisation learns. Updates should be deliberate, versioned and governed, preserving useful history while keeping active knowledge current.

### 11.9 Knowledge Metrics

Knowledge Metrics provide a structured way to assess the health and reliability of DEKS. Metrics should support governance decisions, repository maintenance and Knowledge Debt reduction.

| Metric | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Metadata Completeness | Measures whether Knowledge Assets contain required metadata fields. |
| Review Compliance | Measures whether Knowledge Assets are reviewed according to their required review cycle. |
| Cross-reference Coverage | Measures whether Knowledge Assets include appropriate parent, child, dependency and related references. |
| Knowledge Debt | Tracks missing, outdated, duplicated or low-trust knowledge requiring remediation. |
| Duplicate Assets | Identifies overlapping Knowledge Assets that may require consolidation or clearer authoritative ownership. |
| Orphan Assets | Identifies Knowledge Assets with no clear parent, owner, relationship or repository context. |

## 12 Guiding Principles

1. Knowledge is a strategic asset.

2. Capture Once. Organize Well. Reuse Everywhere.

3. Every Knowledge Asset has one authoritative source.

4. Knowledge should outlive individuals.

5. Documentation exists to enable execution.

6. Research ideas should be captured immediately.

7. AI accelerates work but human judgement remains essential.

8. Enterprise knowledge should continuously evolve.

9. Every Knowledge Asset must have a purpose, owner, metadata and lifecycle.

10. DEKS should function like a well-designed database.

11. Templates should provide structure without preventing thoughtful content.

12. Cross-references should connect knowledge and reduce duplication.

13. The Master Index should provide trusted navigation and discovery.

14. Knowledge Governance is an ongoing responsibility, not a one-time activity.

15. Knowledge Debt should be actively identified and reduced.
